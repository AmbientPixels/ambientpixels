Roadmap

What's in flight, what's queued, and what we're exploring next. We ship in small increments, so this list shifts often.

Snapshot · updated 2026-04-25

Recently shipped Last ~30 days
  • My Forge dashboard — dedicated page (top-nav "Forge") collecting your drafts, published cards, favorites, and decks.
  • Admin KPI strip — cards published, users, hearts, and favorites tiles on the admin panel.
  • Unified global header — one nav across every CardForge page, auth-aware out of the box.
  • Hearts: ratings & favorites — anonymous can heart cards; signed-in users get persistent favorites.
  • Hero fan admin — recent / random / curated / highest-rated splash modes.
  • Highest-rated splash mode — community hearts now drive a hero-fan option.
  • Five new border frames — frost, ember, shadow, nature, circuit.
  • 3-panel obsidian editor — rail nav, click-to-edit on both card faces, ember field, drag-to-pan portraits.
  • Splash landing polish — real-card hero fan, combo ticker, clickable "ways to start" tiles.
  • My Published tabs — see your published cards and decks alongside drafts in editor + deck builder.
Now In flight / immediate next

Visual card-picker for curated splash

Replace the ID-paste curated mode in the hero-fan admin with a visual grid where admins can click to feature cards directly.

Next Queued for the coming sprints

Gallery filters & theme tags

Browse published cards by class, palette, rarity, or theme — with shareable filtered views. Sort filters (recent / rated / all) already live; this layers on faceted filters.

Later Exploring — not committed

Hearts-driven ROI signals

Feed ratings data into the finance dashboards so the platform can tell which campaigns and creators actually resonate with the community.

Optional paid tiers

Higher AI quotas, premium border packs, and other extras — the core creator stays free.

Mobile-friendly editor

A condensed editor experience that works on phones for quick edits and Quick Build flows on the go.

Card collaboration

Share an editor link with someone so two people can iterate on a card together. Very early-stage idea.
