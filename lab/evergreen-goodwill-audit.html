Executive summary

Evergreen Goodwill's website demonstrates genuine strengths in trust-building and differentiation — the named student success stories, specific program outcomes, and multi-county service footprint provide a credible foundation that many nonprofits lack. However, the site currently underperforms in the three dimensions that most directly drive conversion action: CTA strength (41/100), funnel friction (11/100), and conversion hierarchy (29/100), creating a compounding effect where motivated visitors — job seekers, donors, and shoppers — arrive with intent and leave without acting. The most acute exposure is in job training enrolment, where a 'Click Here' label on the primary job seeker entry point is leaving conversion volume on the table daily. (An earlier version of this summary also claimed there was no above-fold donation button. That was wrong: the header carries two.) Based on current messaging clarity and CTA friction scores, Evergreen Goodwill is likely converting 35-55% below potential for a nonprofit of its mission profile and trust signal strength.

Conversion health

A composite score of 56/100 places this site in the category of organizationally credible but structurally under-optimized — common among nonprofits and mission-driven local service organizations that have invested in programs and storytelling but have not applied conversion architecture discipline to the web experience. In practical terms, this score range is associated with significant drop-off before primary conversion action, with industry benchmarks suggesting sites in this range experience 30-50% more abandonment at the decision moment than peers with optimized funnel and CTA structures. For Evergreen Goodwill, where both donation revenue and program enrollment directly fund operations, that abandonment gap represents a measurable and recoverable opportunity.

Start here

Priority 1 Eliminate Navigation and CTA Overload

The combination of 59 navigation links (25 visible at rest, the remainder inside dropdowns) and 14 page-level CTAs is the single largest conversion suppressor on the site. Barry Schwartz's paradox of choice is fully operational here — users presented with this volume of equal-weight options statistically default to inaction. The immediate fix is to collapse the navigation to 5-7 primary items, remove or consolidate redundant CTAs (multiple 'Read More,' 'Contact,' and 'Contact Us' instances), and establish a clear visual hierarchy that guides users toward one primary action per audience segment. The soccer screen-printing event CTA should not compete with job training enrollment on the same page without clear visual separation and priority weighting.

Effort medium · Impact High — reducing choice overload to a focused CTA structure typically increases primary conversion action by 25-45% in comparable nonprofit and local service site environments.

Priority 2 Replace 'Click Here' with an Outcome-Driven Job Seeker CTA

The above-fold CTA for student registration — the primary conversion entry point for the site's core mission audience — currently reads 'Click Here,' which is the lowest-performing CTA language in conversion research, providing no value signal, no outcome, and no reason to act. This should be replaced immediately with outcome-specific language tied to the program benefit, such as 'Start Free Job Training' or 'Apply for Free Job Training Programs.' The word 'free' is a proven conversion accelerant for this audience segment and is already supported by the page copy ('free Job Training and Education programs') — it simply needs to be surfaced at the action moment.

Effort quick · Impact High — replacing non-descriptive CTA language with outcome-specific copy typically increases click-through on primary CTAs by 20-40% without any structural page changes.

Priority 3 Fix the Newsletter Redirect Trust Break

The newsletter signup redirects to donorperfect.net, an unbranded external domain that creates a trust break at the moment of conversion and is frequently read as a phishing risk or a site error. Embedding the form on the page, or white-labelling it to the Evergreen Goodwill domain, removes a stop in front of people who had already decided to act. (This priority previously also called for an above-fold donation button. That was withdrawn: the header already has two.)

Effort medium · Impact Moderate — resolving an external-domain trust break at the point of signup typically reduces abandonment at that step by 20-40%.

Strategic opportunities

  • The four distinct audience segments currently competing for attention on a single homepage — job seekers, donors, shoppers, and employers — each have meaningfully different conversion motivations, trust triggers, and objections. Implementing a segmented entry experience, either through a homepage audience selector or distinct above-fold pathways with audience-specific hero content, would allow Evergreen Goodwill to present the right message to the right visitor at the right moment rather than averaging the message across all four groups and fully serving none of them. This is the highest-leverage structural change available and is expected to produce compounding conversion improvements across all audience segments simultaneously.
  • The student success stories on the page — named individuals with specific, verifiable outcomes including GED completion, journeyman electrician licensure, aviation degrees, and healthcare employment — represent an underutilized conversion asset of significant value. These stories currently function as passive content with 'Read More' links that lead away from the conversion path. Restructuring these stories as a dedicated social proof sequence with embedded CTAs at the emotional peak of each narrative, and testing their use as primary above-fold hero content in rotation, would leverage the site's strongest trust signal directly in the conversion architecture rather than treating it as supplementary content.
