Where the reading happens

To sort a message, this demo sends its sender, subject and first couple of lines to Claude Haiku. Nothing else is sent, and nothing is kept afterwards. That is how this demo is set up, and it is a choice rather than a requirement.

  • Nothing leaves your environmentSorting on sender, domain and patterns alone. No AI reads anything. Good at obvious junk, blind to nuance.
  • Stays inside your Microsoft tenantAzure OpenAI runs in Hanson's own Azure subscription, so message content never crosses your boundary. Nearly the same quality as below.
  • An outside modelthis demoBest judgment, lowest cost, and the messages are read by a service outside your tenant.

Same agent either way. This is a decision about where the reading happens, not a different product, and it can be changed later without rebuilding anything.

What it costs to run

Measured on this demo: 25 messages sorted for about nine tenths of a cent. Sorting email is a comparatively easy job, so tools like this normally run on the small, fast tier rather than the biggest model available.

What I would recommend for Hanson: Azure OpenAI. Not because it is the sharpest or the cheapest — on this job the small models are all close. Because Hanson runs on Microsoft. It sits in your own Azure subscription, under billing and compliance you already have, with no new supplier to approve, and your email never leaves your tenant. That answers the privacy question and the procurement question at the same time.

This demo runs on Claude Haiku because it was the quickest way to show you something real. Moving it is a change of endpoint, not a rebuild.
