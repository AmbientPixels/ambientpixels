The Roast

  • That summary is so generic it could apply to a barista, a Navy SEAL, or a golden retriever. "Results-oriented AND detail-oriented"? You used two filler buzzwords in the same breath. ATS doesn't care, and humans actively cringe.
  • Where are the numbers? You "drove significant improvements" — significant like 2% or significant like you saved the company from collapse? Without metrics, you're just a person who showed up. Quantify everything: revenue, retention, users, velocity, launch timelines. Everything.
  • "Helped drive," "Assisted in," "Participated in" — your bullet points read like someone who was in the building when things happened, not someone who made things happen. This is a Senior PM resume that sounds like an intern's apology letter.
  • "Spearheaded various strategic initiatives" is the corporate equivalent of saying "I did some stuff sometimes." VARIOUS. INITIATIVES. Name them. What product? What outcome? What changed because you existed?
  • Your education section is doing the absolute bare minimum — no relevant coursework, certifications, or even a graduation year that's formatted to show you've kept learning. For a 2017 grad in tech, a lone BA in Communications with zero upskilling signals "coasted."

What's Working

  • Solid 7-year career progression from APM to Senior PM at recognizable companies — the trajectory is genuinely strong and tells a coherent story.
  • Skills section includes real tools (JIRA, Confluence, Figma, SQL) that ATS systems will actually scan for — good instinct there.
  • Consistent formatting and clean structure means a recruiter won't rage-quit before reading it.
  • Staying at Cascade for 5 years across two roles signals loyalty and growth, which hiring managers love to see.

Rewrite Tips

Start by nuking that summary and replacing it with a 2-3 line punch that names your specialty, your scale, and your impact. Something like: "Senior Product Manager with 7 years building B2B SaaS products. Led 0-to-1 launches and scaled features to 50K+ enterprise users at Northwind Digital and Cascade Software Group. Known for turning ambiguous problems into shipped product with measurable outcomes." See how that's different? It's specific, it's confident, and it doesn't insult the reader's intelligence.

For every bullet point, apply the formula: Action Verb + What You Did + Result With a Number. "Spearheaded various strategic initiatives" becomes "Defined and launched 3 core product initiatives in 2023, reducing customer churn by 18% and increasing NPS from 32 to 47." You may need to dig through your memory or old performance reviews, but those numbers exist — go find them. If you truly can't recall exact figures, use ranges or approximations and note them honestly.

Finally, add a certifications or continuing education section. A PSPO, PMC, or even a Google PM certificate signals to ATS and hiring managers that you invest in your craft. Also consider breaking your skills section into categories (Core PM Skills | Tools | Data & Analytics) — it reads faster and scans better for both humans and bots.

Pro Tip

Go to the job descriptions of your top 5 target roles, copy them into a word cloud tool, and find the exact keywords they repeat most. Then audit your resume to make sure those exact phrases appear naturally in your bullets — not just in the skills section. ATS systems are dumb pattern matchers; beat them at their own game before a human ever sees your name.
